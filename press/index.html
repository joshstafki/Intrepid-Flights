Executive Statement — Office of the CEO

Godspeed, Artemis II:
Humanity Returns to the Moon

Intrepid Flights congratulates Commander Reid Wiseman, Pilot Victor Glover, Mission Specialist Christina Koch, and Mission Specialist Jeremy Hansen on the launch of NASA's Artemis II — the first crewed lunar flight in over fifty years.

Hanover, MN — April 1, 2026

Today, at 6:24 p.m. EDT, four extraordinary human beings will climb atop the most powerful operational rocket in the world and do something no crew has done since December of 1972: leave low-Earth orbit and venture toward the Moon. On behalf of everyone at Intrepid Flights, I want to take a moment to honor that.

Aviation and spaceflight share the same irreducible truth: the sky is not a limit — it is an invitation. The men and women of NASA and the Canadian Space Agency who have poured decades of work into the Artemis program represent that truth in its highest form. Today's launch of Artemis II is not simply a milestone for a government agency; it is a milestone for our entire civilization.

"There are four humans that were put in a position to be able to go explore and do something that is very unique and rare in this civilization."

— Commander Reid Wiseman, NASA

Intrepid Flights was built on the conviction that flight — in all its forms — deserves to be celebrated. We exist because we believe that the act of leaving the ground, of trusting in engineering and in human ingenuity, is one of the most profound things a person can do. What Artemis II represents dwarfs anything that happens in our airspace by orders of magnitude, and we are in awe.

Artemis II Crew — April 1, 2026
Reid Wiseman Commander  ·  NASA
Victor Glover Pilot  ·  NASA
Christina Koch Mission Specialist  ·  NASA
Jeremy Hansen Mission Specialist  ·  CSA

Commander Wiseman, Pilot Glover, and Mission Specialist Koch bring deep reserves of spaceflight experience to this mission. Mission Specialist Hansen of the Canadian Space Agency becomes the first non-American to venture beyond low-Earth orbit — a powerful symbol of the international partnership that has always been the backbone of human space exploration at its best.

This ten-day journey around the Moon aboard the Orion spacecraft — named Integrity — is a test mission in the truest sense: a proof of readiness for the Artemis crews who will follow, and ultimately for the astronauts who will one day set foot on the lunar surface again. The goal, as NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman has put it plainly, is not flags and footprints this time. The goal is to stay.

To the entire NASA team, the engineers, the flight directors, the families watching from the ground, and the four souls riding that rocket tonight: you carry our admiration, our prayers, and our deepest respect. The whole sky — and everything beyond it — is cheering for you.

Fly safe. Fly far. Come home.

Issued From the Desk of the CEO
Josh Stafki
